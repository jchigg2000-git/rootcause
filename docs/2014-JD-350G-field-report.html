First-screen diagnosis

Most likely fault chain

  1. Turbocharger-speed sensor, connector, harness, shared reference, or signal plausibility fault.
  2. Actual air-system underperformance: restricted intake, charge-air leak, failed hose/clamp, or biased manifold-pressure/temperature input.
  3. VGT actuator, vane-position feedback, calibration, or sticking turbo geometry.
  4. DPF pressure/temperature feedback or fuel-dosing fault preventing the ECU from achieving or confirming regeneration temperature.
  5. High soot/ash loading created by repeated aborted regens, low-load work, excessive idle, or an engine/EGR fault.

What is still required

  • Exact monitor text and every active/stored SPN/FMI.
  • Engine hours and prior exhaust-filter cleaning history.
  • Service ADVISOR live data during a controlled warm run or attempted parked cleaning.
  • Engine serial/option-code label to verify installed component variants.
  • PIN-specific PIP/campaign and software-calibration check through Deere support.

2. Safety and stop-work warnings

Stop work immediately for a red STOP warning, uncontrolled derate/shutdown, visible exhaust fire, glowing aftertreatment, oil entering the intake/exhaust, severe turbo noise, rapid coolant loss, fuel leakage, or smoke from wiring. Do not continue a parked cleaning in dry vegetation, near combustible material, inside a building without exhaust extraction, or with an active fuel/oil leak.

Before inspection

  • Park on firm level ground; lower bucket/attachment completely.
  • Place pilot shutoff lever in the locked position; apply parking controls.
  • Allow turbocharger and aftertreatment to cool before touching components.
  • Block or support raised structures with approved equipment; hydraulics alone are not support.
  • Disconnect battery power before connector repair or harness replacement, following the machine manual.
  • Use eye protection and gloves; high-pressure fuel and hydraulic injection injuries require emergency care.

During electrical and regen testing

  • Use breakout leads and manufacturer test adapters; do not pierce sealed wiring unless the service procedure specifically requires it.
  • Do not short a sensor-reference circuit to battery voltage or ground.
  • Keep personnel clear of the upperstructure swing radius and tracks.
  • Do not leave a parked cleaning unattended.
  • Do not bypass interlocks, defeat derates, unplug emissions devices to continue work, or alter DEF/DPF/SCR programming.
  • Preserve fault history and freeze-frame data before clearing codes.

The applicable operator manual contains dedicated procedures for parked exhaust-filter cleaning, SCR operation, machine preparation, battery isolation, welding, and connector cleanliness. Confirmed machine fact [1]

Why a turbo-speed fault can disrupt regeneration

  1. The ECU predicts turbo speed from engine speed, fueling, manifold pressure, VGT command, ambient pressure and airflow.
  2. If measured speed is absent, noisy, implausible, or lower than predicted, the ECU cannot safely trust air handling or VGT response.
  3. Active regeneration requires controlled oxygen flow and exhaust heat. The ECU may abort, refuse, prolong or derate the cleaning sequence when air-system control is not credible.
  4. A true low-speed condition also reduces boost, increases smoke/soot, and can load the DPF faster than it can clean.

Reasonable inference The precise inhibit criteria and threshold for this machine are proprietary; the exact SPN/FMI and Service ADVISOR test plan are required.

8. Symptom-based diagnostic paths

A. “Turbo not up to speed” / turbo-speed warning

  1. Make safe and preserve evidence. Lower attachment, lock pilot controls, photograph every message, record every controller/SPN/FMI, occurrence count and time stamp. Do not clear codes.
  2. Identify electrical versus performance FMI. Open/short/missing/invalid-message logic points first to sensor/connector/harness. Predicted-versus-measured or low-value logic requires both circuit checks and airflow/VGT testing.
  3. Cold visual inspection. Inspect sensor lead at turbo, connector lock, terminal tension, oil/water intrusion, melted loom, abrasion at clamps, and harness routing near exhaust/turbo surfaces.
  4. Use the exact schematic. From TM13206X19/engine CTM, identify supply, return, signal, splice and ECU pins. Test with breakout leads; compare sensor return to ECU return, not merely chassis.
  5. Graph live data. Turbo speed must respond smoothly with engine load and correlate with VGT command, MAP, barometric pressure and engine speed. Dropouts or impossible jumps while other values remain stable indicate signal trouble.
  6. Prove the air path. Inspect/pressure-test air cleaner outlet, both compressor stages, CAC, hoses and clamps; evaluate restriction and MAP/IAT plausibility.
  7. Prove VGT control. Run commanded actuator sweep/calibration. Compare commanded and actual position. Stop if abnormal mechanical noise, seizure or oil leakage is found.
  8. Only then evaluate mechanical turbo condition. Use approved wheel/shaft/oil-feed tests and account for downstream exhaust restriction.

B. Parked cleaning starts but takes too long or aborts

  1. Confirm no turbo/air-handling, fuel, coolant, DPF, dosing, DEF/SCR or network fault is active; many active faults prevent a valid cleaning.
  2. Verify prerequisites from the operator monitor/manual: safe parking state, adequate fluids/fuel/DEF, correct switch setting, engine at required conditions, pilot controls locked, and auto cleaning not intentionally inhibited.
  3. Graph DOC inlet/outlet, DPF and SCR-related temperatures. Determine whether the ECU commands heat and whether each sensor rises smoothly in the expected sequence.
  4. If temperature does not rise, test dosing command/pressure, exhaust leaks, DOC response, VGT/EGR/air-throttle control and engine temperature.
  5. If temperature rises but restriction remains high, validate DPF pressure sensor/hoses, soot estimate and ash load. Ash will not burn away.
  6. If cleaning completes but repeats quickly, diagnose the soot source: intake leak/restriction, EGR, injector/rail pressure, oil consumption, low-load duty cycle or biased sensors.

C. DEF/SCR warning or derate appears with regen complaint

  1. Separate DPF cleaning codes from SCR inducement codes; they are linked systems but different failure paths.
  2. Confirm DEF level, age, contamination, concentration and freeze/thaw status with approved tools.
  3. Because S/N 810000 is in the early header range, check whether the suction screen/baffle service was performed and whether pressure/purge are normal.
  4. Inspect heated lines, connectors, dosing valve and crystallization; run supply/dosing tests with Service ADVISOR.
  5. Evaluate NOx sensor power/network/plausibility and exhaust leaks. Replace no NOx sensor solely from a generic “regen” complaint.

D. Low power, smoke or abnormal turbo noise

Electrical/signal pattern
Intermittent turbo-speed dropouts, stable MAP/engine operation, harness movement changes fault.
OR
True airflow/mechanical pattern
Low MAP, smoke, slow turbo response, charge-air leak, VGT mismatch, oil/noise or high exhaust restriction.

Do not perform a parked cleaning with severe turbo noise, oil smoke, suspected compressor contact, or an uncontrolled fuel/oil leak. A hot regeneration can worsen a fire or runaway hazard.

10. Repair-priority plan

  1. Immediate safety and damage prevention — owner/operator. Stop for severe turbo noise, oil smoke, red STOP, fuel/oil leakage, wiring smoke or exhaust fire risk. Do not force regeneration around combustibles.
  2. Free visual and operating checks — informed owner/operator. Photograph messages; record exact codes; check auto-clean setting, DEF/fuel level, air-restriction indicator, cooler cleanliness, CAC hoses/clamps, obvious exhaust leaks, turbo sensor connector and harness rub points.
  3. Basic maintenance and contamination checks — owner or technician. Verify correct air/fuel filters and oil; drain water separators; assess DEF quality and early serial-range header service; inspect battery terminals/grounds.
  4. Fault-code and live-data review — trained technician with Service ADVISOR. Preserve all controller codes and snapshots. Graph turbo speed, VGT command/actual, MAP/baro/IAT, DPF pressure, exhaust temperatures, soot/ash estimate, dosing and SCR data.
  5. Targeted electrical/airflow tests — trained technician. Use TM13206X19, breakout leads and approved pressure/smoke-test equipment. Verify sensor circuit, harness, grounds, charge-air integrity and VGT sweep/calibration.
  6. Aftertreatment tests — emissions-trained technician. Validate DPF tubes/sensor, temperature sensors, fuel doser/DOC heat response, DEF supply/doser/heaters and NOx plausibility. Avoid blind sensor replacement.
  7. Software and PIN-specific checks — Deere dealer or authorized service. Check active PIPs, calibration versions, parts supersessions and required reset/relearn procedures.
  8. Removal or major repair — qualified shop. Remove DPF for flow/ash cleaning only after sensor and root-cause proof. Remove turbo only after circuit, air-path, VGT, lubrication and restriction tests isolate mechanical failure.

Owner/operator can reasonably do

  • Record codes/messages and maintenance history.
  • Inspect accessible hoses, clamps, loom and connectors while cool and powered down.
  • Clean cooler exterior with approved method.
  • Verify approved fluids and DEF handling.
  • Check whether automatic cleaning has been disabled.

Requires technician/tools

  • Backprobe/breakout electrical measurements.
  • Service ADVISOR live-data graphing and actuator tests.
  • VGT calibration and software updates.
  • Charge-air pressure/smoke test.
  • DPF flow/ash evaluation, doser tests and DEF pressure tests.
  • Turbo removal or aftertreatment disassembly.

Rejected or downgraded evidence
